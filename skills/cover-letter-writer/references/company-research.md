# Company Research

## What To Research

Always research the company before drafting unless the user explicitly says not to.

Look for:

- Product, service, and customer segment
- Industry and business model
- Public values or mission
- Engineering, design, or operating culture
- Tone in careers pages, job ads, and public communications
- Recent credible news only when relevant

Use current sources when facts may have changed.

## Overlap Mapping

Before drafting, create a private mental map between company signals and the user's source material:

- Company values to CV evidence, personal information, or recommendation-backed traits
- Product or customer context to relevant domain experience
- Role priorities to concrete skills, projects, or working habits
- Culture signals to collaboration style, reliability, learning ability, or communication evidence

Only use overlaps that are supported by the user's material. Do not invent motivation, passion, domain experience, or personal values.

## How To Use Research

Use research to shape emphasis, not to add a separate values paragraph.

Prefer subtle alignment:

- Connect the user's real experience to the company's product context.
- Mirror the ad's priorities in natural wording.
- Mention the company only where it makes the letter more specific.
- Bring out values overlap through concrete examples, not slogans.

Avoid:

- Listing company values mechanically
- Overpraising the company
- Claiming personal passion without evidence
- Depending on stale or uncertain facts

## If Research Is Blocked

If web access is unavailable, use company details present in the ad and say that external company research could not be completed. Do not pretend external research was done.
